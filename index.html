Where the views came from.

Not luck, and not posting more by hand. Data first, then scripts, then cuts.

  • Every hit, downloaded. I pulled every video of ours that passed 100,000 views, up to the ones with tens of millions, with full transcripts and the complete YouTube statistics.
  • An agent read all of it. It compared what held people and what lost them, and showed which topics, hooks and structures worked.
  • I wrote the scripts from that. We shot them twice over: long horizontal videos for YouTube and separate Reels. The Reels brought millions of views on their own.
  • Every long video was cut again. Vertical clips went out on every platform, which brought more views than the originals.
  • Now it tests itself. Each account runs five to eight trial Reels at a time, and the winners get pushed.

The content engine.

Built for a resale brand with five Instagram accounts. I wrote the brand book, then turned it into code: type, layout, voice and forty rules the engine checks before anyone sees a slide.

  • It reads. Bots watch the blogs and sources the brand trusts and pull the stories worth a carousel, with their files.
  • The team picks. The shortlist lands in the work chat. The topics with the most reactions go to production.
  • It builds. Carousels at 1080×1350 in the brand's voice, three covers to choose from, the post text and hashtags in one folder.
  • It publishes. Approved carousels go to Instagram on schedule, on topics you set or on what is trending this week.
  • It learns. Every correction a person makes becomes a rule, so next week needs fewer of them.
